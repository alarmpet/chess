# A. Commercial card collection and crafting economies

Survey date 2026-10-31. Scope: acquisition and pity, duplicates, craft and dismantle ratios, completion motivation, player reaction.
Plan and README were read first. This file corrects them where noted and does not repeat them.

## How to read this

**Source marks**

| Mark | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| O | Official publisher page, read directly |
| O* | Official statement, seen only through a wiki or press report |
| W | Community wiki |
| F | Fan site, guide, press or forum post |
| U | Single weak source, or sources conflict |
| NF | Not found. No number is given |

**Limits**

- Numbers are dated by their source. Live-service values change. Many F sources are from 2019 to 2025.
- These pages refused automated reading, so their content here comes from search snippets or other sources: Bulbapedia, Yugipedia, Pokémon Zone, VGC, Prydwen, Mobalytics, PokéBeach, all Fandom wikis, Blizzard support.
- Reddit was not read directly. Reddit opinion appears only as reported by press or mirrors.
- Korean community coverage is thin. Two Ruliweb posts were read. Inven and DCinside gave no directly relevant thread.
- Player reactions are paraphrased, not quoted, apart from one short press quote. This follows a copyright limit on quoting.

## 7. Corrections to the plan and README

1. **Plan §9 credits the wrong game.** It says duplicates become dust that buys cards, "Pokémon TCG Pocket's exchange structure". In Pocket, duplicates give Shinedust, which buys flair and trades. Cards are bought with Pack Points, earned per pack regardless of duplicates. The plan's loop is the Hearthstone, Gwent, Shadowverse model.
2. **README Hearthstone row.** The wiki states Blizzard published exact pity numbers on 2023-03-14. "Disputed documentation" is out of date. The primary Blizzard post was not reached.
3. **Pity precedent is sound.** Showing odds and a counter matches Arena's published odds and EA's in-store odds. Arena's hidden Vault is the one to avoid.

## 8. Patterns that work

- **Duplicate protection inside a rarity.** Hearthstone's rule drew repeated praise.
- **A guaranteed early high.** Hearthstone's first Legendary within 10 packs, Arknights' 5★ within 10 pulls.
- **Currency that always pays.** Pocket's 5 points per pack and Arena's track reward effort whatever the luck.
- **Choosing the exact card.** Dust, wildcards, tokens, spark and FEH's free focus hero all end bad luck the same way.
- **Duplicates that never read as nothing.** Almost every surveyed game with duplicates turns the excess into something.
- **Cosmetic-only upgrades.** Snap proves collection depth without stat power, matching the plan's rule.
- **Late sinks.** Pocket flair and Snap Infinity splits absorb currency after a collection is finished.
- **Published rates.** Arena and EA publish theirs, so the odds can be checked.
- **Gaps that hint at an action.** Neko Atsume's cats need specific toys. Animal Crossing's entries suggest what to catch next.

## 9. Patterns players hate

- **Destroying good cards as a currency.** Pocket's Trade Tokens and EA's SBC fodder. Pocket withdrew tokens.
- **A second currency that needs a sacrifice before a feature works.** Same case.
- **Crafting that a later change voids.** Arena rebalances, Clash Royale's Elite Wild Cards.
- **Crafted card then pulled again.** Master Duel players plan around this.
- **Permanent deletion with no undo.** My inference from FEH's send-home warning, not a measured complaint.
- **Surplus low currency with no outlet.** Arena's common wildcards.
- **Losing after reaching pity.** The 50/50 is the sharpest complaint in gacha.
- **Too much content for the income.** EA SBC volume, Snap's newest series.
- **Hidden progress.** Arena's Vault shows nothing until full.
- **Free elsewhere, still in the paid pool.** Hearthstone's Alterac cards.

## 10. What fits a free solo cute chess game

The arithmetic below is mine and uses the plan's simulation values. It is not measured on people.

**Duplicate protection matters more than dust.** Six hero slots with random repeats take 6 × (1 + 1/2 + ... + 1/6) ≈ 14.7 heroes on average. With protection they take 6. At the plan's rough 6.5 games per hero, that is about 96 games against 39.

**Dust as drafted does little for heroes.** Take Hearthstone's price of 1600 and an assumed drop mix of 70/20/8/2.

| State | Dust per game | Hero craft |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Everything is a duplicate, late game | about 150 | about 11 games |
| Only commons and advanced repeat, heroes protected | about 48 | about 33 games |

Protected drops finish the hero row by about game 39, so dust will rarely decide a hero. Use it for targeted rare cards and for a sink after completion.

**Recommendations, as prototype starting values**

1. Protect rare and hero drops inside the rarity. Let commons repeat so dust still flows. When a rarity is full, repeats become dust.
2. Convert duplicates automatically at 25% of craft price. Offer no manual dismantle, so nothing can be regretted.
3. Start prices at 40 / 100 / 400 / 1600, then retune from the measured capture log. Do not ship before Step 0 data exists.
4. Exchanging a card should not count against pity, and buying a card should remove it from random drops. This avoids the Master Duel regret.
5. Add one early guarantee: first rare or better within the first finished game. The plan's child case waits 10.7 games for a hero.
6. Plan a sink, because the 24-card album can finish in about 40 games. Cosmetic options are alternate card finishes, village display items and frames. Keep them cosmetic.
7. Keep the pity counter visible. Make hero pity a true guarantee of an unowned hero, which avoids the 50/50 feeling.
8. Show why each empty slot is empty, such as "capture a rook to raise your hero odds". Keep the tone of Neko Atsume.
9. Do not copy these: daily pack limits, stamina, friend trading, rotation, card-burning SBCs, tokens that need sacrifice, or dupes that add power.
10. Pair each set reward with a hard stop. Set completion is a less proven pattern here, so test one animal first, as Step 7 already says.

**Scope questions for the plan**

- Common rows saturate fast. 14.7 commons are about 3 games at 6.4 cards a game, if 70% are common. After that most drops are dust, so the reveal needs a duplicate treatment that still feels good.
- The plan counts 24 art cards but lists 8 effect cards. Decide whether effect cards are album entries, because that changes every number above.
