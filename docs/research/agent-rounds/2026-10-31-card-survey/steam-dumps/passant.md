I want to like this game.

I really, really want to like this game.

I love chess riffs. I've played Really Bad Chess every day for the last six years. Pawnbarian and Shotgun King are my all-timers. I effing *love* chess riffs.

I *should* like this game. I *should* love this game.

But I don't.

There are serious interface issues with the semi-3D board. When I select a piece it should always be the piece I intended to select; it isn't. When I move my piece it should always go to the place I intend; it doesn't. The board is just wonky enough to induce unintentional errors.

The stakes should noticeably increase with each level; they don't. The bonuses should give me a slight edge over my opponent, and each new badge should get you a little more power; instead, the right badge in round one lets you run ramshod, and even the wrong badge is overpowered.

It's chess but Balatro but Balatro works because poker is fast. Chess is not fast, but Passant makes it an utter slog. Roguelike/deckbuilding takes on traditional games need to make the more fun, but somehow Passant makes it less fun.

I really wanted to like this game. Honest.

### R19 · Recommended · 7.8 hrs on record · 5 July · english/trendsixmonths
Turning chess into a roguelite is a tough premise, because the skill difference in chess players is huge. If your game is 95% chess then grandmaster level players will breeze through your game without having to interact with your roguelike mechanics at all, and at that point they might aswell play chess. If the roguelike mechanics are so powerful that chess noobs can rely on them for support, and playing correct moves is no longer important, then your game becomes just about the metagame and the chess is trivialized, and then why pick chess as the base for your roguelite and not something simpler?

At the end of the day Passant suffers from the same issue, but until you've figured out all the mechanics it's actually a great game. You have to play decent chess in the early game, where material is equal, and you have to interact with roguelike mechanics because your opponents will have 70 points of material in the later game. Once you've figured out which items are good then it's just chess again though, so for me it's back to chess.com.

All in all 7€ well spent though -- Passant is probably my favourite chess roguelite, on par with Usurper.
